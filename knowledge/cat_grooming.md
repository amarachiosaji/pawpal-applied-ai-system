# Cat Grooming Guidelines

Short-haired cats typically need brushing once or twice a week to reduce
shedding and hairball formation, while long-haired breeds (Persians, Maine
Coons, Ragdolls) require daily brushing to prevent matting, particularly
around the armpits, belly, and behind the ears where fur tangles first.

Most cats groom themselves effectively and rarely need baths, but bathing may
be necessary for cats with mobility issues (arthritis, obesity) who can't
reach all areas, or if their coat becomes soiled with something that
self-grooming can't remove. Nail trims are generally needed every 2–3 weeks;
overgrown nails can curl into the paw pad and cause pain or infection.

Senior cats and overweight cats often lose flexibility and struggle to groom
hard-to-reach spots like the lower back and hindquarters, making them prone to
matting and skin issues in those areas — increased brushing frequency helps
compensate. Watch for excessive scratching, bald patches, or skin redness,
which can indicate allergies, parasites, or dental pain being misattributed to
grooming issues.

Never use dog shampoo or human hair products on cats, as many contain
ingredients (like certain essential oils) that are toxic to cats due to their
limited ability to metabolize them.
