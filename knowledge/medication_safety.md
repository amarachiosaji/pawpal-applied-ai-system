# Medication Administration and Safety

Medications should generally be given at consistent times each day, since
many drugs (particularly for chronic conditions like thyroid or seizure
management) rely on stable blood concentration levels to work effectively.
Missing doses or giving them at wildly inconsistent times can reduce efficacy
or, for some medications, trigger withdrawal-like effects.

Many oral medications are best given with food to reduce stomach upset, but
some — particularly certain thyroid medications and a few antibiotics — need
to be given on an empty stomach for proper absorption. Always check whether a
specific medication has food timing requirements rather than assuming food is
always fine.

Never give human medications to pets without explicit veterinary guidance.
Common over-the-counter human drugs such as ibuprofen and acetaminophen are
toxic to dogs and cats even in small doses, and this is one of the most common
causes of accidental pet poisoning.

If a dose is missed, do not automatically double the next dose to "catch up" —
this can cause overdose-related side effects. Contact a veterinarian for
missed-dose guidance specific to the medication involved. Keep a written or
digital log of administration times, especially for pets on multiple
medications, to avoid accidental double-dosing between household members.
