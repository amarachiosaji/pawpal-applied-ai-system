# Dog Walking Guidelines

Adult dogs generally need 30–60 minutes of walking per day, which can be split
into one or two sessions. High-energy breeds such as Border Collies, Huskies,
and retrievers often need closer to the upper end of that range, or additional
off-leash play, to avoid boredom-driven destructive behavior. Small or
low-energy breeds may be adequately exercised with two shorter 15-minute walks.

Puppies under six months should follow the "five-minute rule": roughly five
minutes of structured walking per month of age, twice a day, to avoid stressing
still-developing joints. Senior dogs (typically seven years or older, earlier
for large breeds) often benefit from shorter, more frequent walks rather than
one long outing, since arthritis and reduced stamina are common.

Brachycephalic (flat-faced) breeds like Bulldogs and Pugs are prone to
overheating and breathing difficulty, and should avoid walks during high heat
or humidity. For any dog, watch for signs of overexertion: excessive panting
that doesn't subside with rest, lagging behind, limping, or reluctance to
continue. These are signals to shorten or end the walk immediately.

Morning and evening walks are generally preferable to midday walks in hot
climates, both to avoid pavement burns on paw pads and heat exhaustion.
