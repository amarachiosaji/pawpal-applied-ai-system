# Feeding Schedules and Nutrition Timing

Adult dogs typically do well on two meals per day, roughly 8–12 hours apart,
to maintain stable blood sugar and avoid the digestive upset associated with
one large meal. Puppies under six months usually need three to four smaller
meals daily due to their higher metabolic needs and smaller stomach capacity.

Cats are natural "little and often" eaters; free-feeding dry food or splitting
wet food into three to four small portions throughout the day mimics their
natural hunting pattern better than one or two large meals. However, free-
feeding can contribute to obesity in indoor cats with low activity levels, so
portion control matters more than frequency for weight management.

Always ensure fresh water is available at all times regardless of feeding
schedule — dehydration risk is separate from and just as important as
feeding frequency. Water bowls should be cleaned daily, as bacterial buildup
can discourage pets from drinking enough.

Avoid feeding immediately before or after vigorous exercise; waiting at least
30–60 minutes on either side reduces the risk of bloat, which is a serious and
sometimes fatal condition especially in large, deep-chested dog breeds.
