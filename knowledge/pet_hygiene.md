# General Pet Hygiene and Litter Box Care

Litter boxes should be scooped at least once daily, and ideally twice for
multi-cat households, since cats are prone to avoiding a dirty box entirely
and may urinate elsewhere instead. A full box change (dumping all litter,
washing the box, refilling) should happen every 1–2 weeks for clumping
litter, more often for non-clumping types.

The general rule of thumb is one litter box per cat, plus one extra, since
cats can be territorial about sharing. Boxes should be placed in quiet,
low-traffic areas away from food and water bowls, as cats are instinctively
reluctant to eliminate near where they eat.

Sudden changes in litter box habits — avoiding the box, urinating just
outside it, or noticeably increased or decreased frequency — can signal a
urinary tract infection, stress, or a territorial dispute with another pet,
and are worth mentioning at the next vet visit rather than assumed to be
purely behavioral.

For dogs, general hygiene includes wiping paws after walks (especially in
wet or salted/treated winter conditions), regular ear checks for odor or
discharge (particularly in floppy-eared breeds prone to infections), and
routine teeth brushing to prevent tartar buildup, which is one of the most
overlooked aspects of home pet care.
